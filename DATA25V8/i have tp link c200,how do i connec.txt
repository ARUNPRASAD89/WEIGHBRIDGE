f course. This is a very common and practical setup. I can guide you through the exact steps to connect your TP-Link C200 camera to your Python project, especially considering your network setup with the Huawei E8372.

The key to making this work is a protocol called RTSP (Real-Time Streaming Protocol). Your goal is to enable this on your camera and find its unique stream URL.

Here is the complete, step-by-step plan.

The Big Picture: How Your Devices Connect
Huawei E8372: This device creates your local Wi-Fi network.
TP-Link C200 & Your PC: Both your camera and your computer must be connected to the same Wi-Fi network created by the Huawei modem.
Python Script: Your Python code will use the camera's local IP address to tap into its video stream.
This is a perfect setup for a local project.

Step-by-Step Guide to Connect Your Camera
Step 1: Find Your Camera's IP Address
You need to know the local IP address of your C200 camera. You can find this in two main ways:

In the Tapo App:

Open the Tapo app on your phone.
Tap on your C200 camera to view its live feed.
Tap the gear icon (⚙️) in the top-right corner to go to its settings.
Tap on the camera's name at the top of the settings page.
You will see an "IP Address" field (e.g., 192.168.8.101). Write this IP address down.
In Your Huawei Modem's Admin Panel:

Connect to your Huawei Wi-Fi.
Open a web browser and go to 192.168.8.1.
Log in (the default password is often admin or on a sticker on the device).
Look for a "Connected Devices," "DHCP Clients," or "Statistics" section. You will see a list of all devices on your network. Find the one named "Tapo C200" or similar and note its IP address.
Step 2: Enable the RTSP Stream in the Tapo App (This is the most important step!)
For security, TP-Link makes you create a separate username and password specifically for the video stream.

Open the Tapo app and go to your C200's settings (gear icon ⚙️).
Scroll down and tap on Advanced Settings.
Tap on Camera Account.
You will see a screen to create a username and password. Do not use your TP-Link cloud login here. Create a new, simple username and password that your Python script will use.
Example Username: pycam
Example Password: stream123
Save this account. You have now enabled the RTSP stream.
Step 3: Construct the RTSP URL
The RTSP URL for TP-Link cameras has a specific format. There are two versions: a high-definition (HD) stream and a standard-definition (SD) stream.

HD Stream (1080p): stream1
SD Stream (360p): stream2 (This is often better for computer vision as it uses less CPU power).
Using the information from the previous steps, build your URL like this:

rtsp://<USERNAME>:<PASSWORD>@<CAMERA_IP_ADDRESS>:554/stream2

Let's use our examples:

Username: pycam
Password: stream123
IP Address: 192.168.8.101
Your final RTSP URL would be: rtsp://pycam:stream123@192.168.8.101:554/stream2

Step 4: Write the Python Code
Now you can use this URL in your Python script with the OpenCV library. This code will connect to your camera and show you the live feed.
CAMERA TEST.PY
import cv2

# --- Configuration ---
# Paste the RTSP URL you constructed in the previous step here.
RTSP_URL = 'rtsp://pycam:stream123@192.168.8.101:554/stream2'

# It's a good practice to set these environment variables for some network setups
import os
os.environ['OPENCV_FFMPEG_CAPTURE_OPTIONS'] = 'rtsp_transport;udp'

# --- Main Program ---
print("Connecting to camera stream...")
# Use cv2.CAP_FFMPEG to ensure the correct backend is used.
cap = cv2.VideoCapture(RTSP_URL, cv2.CAP_FFMPEG)

if not cap.isOpened():
    print("Error: Could not open video stream.")
    print("Please check the following:")
    print("1. Is the camera powered on and connected to the same Wi-Fi as the PC?")
    print("2. Is the RTSP_URL correct (IP, username, password)?")
    print("3. Is RTSP enabled in the Tapo app with a Camera Account?")
    exit()

print("Stream connected successfully! Press 'q' to quit.")

while True:
    # Read one frame from the video stream
    ret, frame = cap.read()
    
    # ret will be False if the stream ends or has an error
    if not ret:
        print("Error reading frame from stream. Reconnecting...")
        cap.release()
        cap = cv2.VideoCapture(RTSP_URL, cv2.CAP_FFMPEG)
        continue

    # --- YOUR COMPUTER VISION LOGIC GOES HERE ---
    # For example, you would pass this 'frame' to your license plate recognition function.
    # For now, we will just display the live feed.
    cv2.imshow('TP-Link C200 Feed', frame)

    # Wait for 1 millisecond, and check if the 'q' key was pressed
    if cv2.waitKey(1) & 0xFF == ord('q'):
        break

# --- Cleanup ---
print("Closing stream and windows.")
cap.release()
cv2.destroyAllWindows()
